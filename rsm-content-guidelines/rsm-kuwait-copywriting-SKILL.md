# RSM in Kuwait: Copywriting and Editorial Skill

This skill governs every piece of written content produced for RSM in Kuwait. Follow all rules here before writing, reviewing, or approving any copy. Do not rely on intuition or general writing knowledge. This guideline is the authority.

---

## 1. The Golden Rules (Non-Negotiable)

These six rules override everything else. If in doubt, return here.

1. **Always write "RSM in Kuwait".** Never "RSM Kuwait", never "RSM-Kuwait", never abbreviate to "RSM KW". The full form is always used.
2. **Never use a comma before "and".** This applies everywhere: lists, compound sentences and transitions. No Oxford comma.
3. **Do not oversell, exaggerate or over-justify.** Every sentence must be able to stand in front of a senior government official or a professional standards body without sounding promotional.
4. **Adjectives must earn their place.** If an adjective does not add factual precision, remove it. When in doubt, remove it.
5. **Let data speak.** Numbers, percentages and evidence establish credibility. Adjectives do not.
6. **Subtle is the standard.** Communications should read the way a well-regarded professional body or government ministry writes: calm, measured and confident without being self-congratulatory.

---

## 2. Brand Voice in Plain Terms

RSM in Kuwait communicates the way a senior partner in a respected professional services firm speaks to a client:

- **Not** like a brand trying to win business.
- **Not** like a marketing agency promoting a product.
- **Not** like a startup building hype.

The tone is:

| IS | IS NOT |
|---|---|
| Measured | Promotional |
| Credible | Self-congratulatory |
| Collaborative | Assertive or dominant |
| Reflective | Overconfident |
| Professional and clear | Buzzword-heavy |
| Calm | Emotional or enthusiastic |
| Precise | Padded with adjectives |

## 3. Phrasing Conventions: The Definitive Reference

### 3.1 Future Intent
Never promise. Always express intent or direction.

| ✅ Use | ❌ Avoid |
|---|---|
| We aim to | We will |
| We plan to | We ensure |
| We continue to | We guarantee |
| We work towards | We will deliver |
| We seek to | We will achieve |
| We remain focused on | We promise |

### 3.2 Service Quality
Let consistency and reliability describe service, not superlatives.

| ✅ Use | ❌ Avoid |
|---|---|
| Consistent | High-quality |
| Reliable | Outstanding |
| Steady | Superior |
| Measured | Best |
| Dependable | Exceptional |

### 3.3 Expertise
Describe experience factually. Never claim dominance or superiority.

| ✅ Use | ❌ Avoid |
|---|---|
| Experienced | Strong |
| Knowledgeable | Leading |
| Specialised | Unmatched |
| Practised | Exceptional |
| Familiar with | World-class |

### 3.4 Client Relationship Language
Collaboration and support, not conquest or performance.

| ✅ Use | ❌ Avoid |
|---|---|
| Collaborative | Proactive (as a self-description) |
| Supportive | Aggressive |
| Timely | Assertive |
| Clear | Dominant |
| Responsive | Winning |

### 3.5 Client Trust
Express value as something built over time, not claimed or marketed.

| ✅ Use | ❌ Avoid |
|---|---|
| We value client trust | We earn loyalty |
| We seek to build confidence | We win trust |
| We remain committed to | We secure relationships |
| We continue to strengthen | We lock in |

### 3.6 Improvement and Performance
Frame gaps constructively and results modestly.

| ✅ Use | ❌ Avoid |
|---|---|
| Areas for growth | Weaknesses |
| Opportunities for enhancement | Failures |
| Scope for improvement | Gaps to fix |
| Steady progress | Dramatic improvement |
| Encouraging results | Exceptional results |

## 4. Words: The Master Reference

### 4.1 Preferred Vocabulary Palette

These words reflect RSM in Kuwait's measured and credible voice:

> aim, plan, continue, develop, maintain, build, strengthen, enhance, reflect, indicate, support, collaborate, engage, learn, adapt, improve, understand, measure, assess, consider, explore, review, monitor, sustain, advance, clarify, address, note, observe

### 4.2 Words to Avoid: Full List

| Category | Banned Words |
|---|---|
| Overpromising | ensure, guarantee, will achieve, will deliver, will secure, promise, will transform |
| Superlatives | best, leading, world-class, unmatched, exceptional, premium, top, #1, foremost |
| Exaggeration | outstanding, unparalleled, remarkable, extraordinary, impressive, phenomenal, incredible |
| Aggression | strong, dominant, proactive (self-referential), assertive, powerful, aggressive, bold |
| Buzzwords | cutting-edge, innovative (unless describing a specific product feature), groundbreaking, game-changing, next-level, disruptive |
| Intensifiers | very, highly, extremely, deeply, truly, absolutely, significantly (when used to exaggerate rather than quantify) |
| Clichés | out-of-the-box, best-in-class, thought leader, synergy, leverage (as a verb), holistic, seamless (unless literal) |
| Self-congratulatory | proud to announce, delighted to share (unless celebrating a client or partner achievement), privileged |

## 5. Grammar and Punctuation Rules

1. **No Oxford comma (serial comma).** Write "reports, proposals and presentations" not "reports, proposals, and presentations".
2. **Active voice preferred.** Write "We reviewed the findings" not "The findings were reviewed".
3. **Short sentences over long ones.** If a sentence exceeds 25 words, consider splitting it.
4. **No exclamation marks** in professional copy. Ever.
5. **Do not use em-dashes** in any RSM in Kuwait content. They are not permitted in any format or channel. Use a colon, a full stop, a comma or reword the sentence instead.
6. **Numbers under 10 are written out**, unless in a data context (e.g., scores, percentages, years).
7. **Percentage format.** Write as % in data contexts. Spell out "per cent" in flowing prose if preferred by the client's style.
8. **Avoid passive constructions that obscure accountability.** "It was decided" should become "The team decided" or "We decided".

## 6. Structural Writing Standards

### 6.1 Openings
Begin with purpose and context, never with a claim of greatness.

✅ **Correct:**
> "This report presents an analysis of client feedback collected between July 2024 and June 2025 to assess performance and identify areas for growth."

❌ **Incorrect:**
> "This report showcases our exceptional performance and industry leadership in client satisfaction."

### 6.2 Transitions and Connectors
Use reflective and logical connectors, not assertive or conclusive ones.

✅ **Use:**
- As part of this approach…
- In line with ongoing efforts…
- The results indicate that…
- This suggests that clients generally…
- Building on this…
- In this context…

❌ **Avoid:**
- This clearly proves…
- These results confirm beyond doubt…
- This demonstrates our superiority in…
- Evidently…

### 6.3 Closings and Conclusions
End on a note of reflection, gratitude or continued commitment, not self-promotion.

✅ **Correct:**
> "We appreciate our clients' continued engagement and remain focused on using their feedback to strengthen our collaboration."

❌ **Incorrect:**
> "We will continue to lead the market and deliver the best service available."

## 7. Data and Results Language

Data must always speak for itself. Do not frame numbers as proof or evidence of superiority.

| ✅ Correct Framing | ❌ Incorrect Framing |
|---|---|
| "A CSAT of 97% reflects steady service quality." | "A CSAT of 97% confirms our outstanding service excellence." |
| "A CES of 4.6 suggests clients generally find it easy to work with us." | "A CES of 4.6 proves our world-class client experience." |
| "An NPS of 62% indicates a healthy base of client advocates." | "An NPS of 62% highlights our leadership in client loyalty." |
| "Response times improved by 12% over the period." | "Response times saw dramatic, exceptional improvement." |

## 8. Content Type: Tone Calibration

Different formats require slightly different calibrations within the same overall voice.

| Content Type | Tone Notes |
|---|---|
| **Reports & Publications** | Most formal. Objective, analytical and data-driven. No emotional tone. No promotional phrasing anywhere. |
| **Client Proposals** | Consultative. Use "We recommend" and "We propose". Never use "We promise" or "We guarantee". |
| **Advisory Briefs** | Authoritative but measured. Present analysis and options, not directives or sales pitches. |
| **Presentations** | Clean, minimal, factual. Every slide point should be a statement of fact or considered view, not a marketing headline. |
| **Internal Communications** | Engaging but still factual. "We plan to implement" not "We will transform." |
| **Social Media & External Posts** | Warm, appreciative and confident, not superior. Celebrate outcomes, not dominance. Verbs: share, recognise, reflect, mark, note. Not: lead, dominate, achieve, win. |
| **Email Campaigns** | Clear subject lines without hyperbole. Body copy should be direct and purposeful. No "Exciting news!" or "We're thrilled to announce." |
| **Brochures & Factsheets** | Factual and structured. Lead with service descriptions and client benefits, not firm achievements. |
| **Webinar Materials** | Educational and collaborative in tone. Invite participation. Do not lecture or promote. |
| **Press Releases** | Neutral and newsy. State what happened, who was involved and what it means. No promotional framing. |

## 9. Common Mistakes: Before and After Reference

These are the most frequent errors in RSM in Kuwait copy. Use this table when reviewing.

| Before (Incorrect) | After (Corrected) |
|---|---|
| "We deliver strong, high-quality services that make us a leader in our field." | "We focus on providing reliable and consistent services that meet client needs." |
| "Our firm ensures the best results through unmatched expertise." | "Our firm aims to achieve effective outcomes through experience and collaboration." |
| "We are proud of our exceptional performance and market leadership." | "Our results reflect steady progress and a commitment to ongoing improvement." |
| "RSM Kuwait has built a highly successful track record." | "RSM in Kuwait has built a consistent track record across its areas of practice." |
| "This cutting-edge approach ensures superior client outcomes." | "This approach supports clearer and more consistent client outcomes." |
| "We are thrilled to announce our innovative new service." | "We are pleased to introduce a new service designed to support clients with…" |
| "Our world-class team of experts guarantees excellence." | "Our team brings specialised experience across relevant areas of practice." |
| "The results confirm our outstanding achievement." | "The results reflect progress across the areas measured." |
| "Clients, partners, and stakeholders agree." | "Clients, partners and stakeholders agree." *(remove comma before "and")* |

## 10. Proofreading Checklist

When reviewing any RSM in Kuwait content, run through this checklist:

- [ ] Does the copy say "RSM in Kuwait" (not "RSM Kuwait") every time?
- [ ] Is there a comma before "and" anywhere? Remove all instances.
- [ ] Are there superlatives or exaggerations? (best, leading, world-class, unmatched, exceptional, etc.)
- [ ] Are there promises or guarantees? (will, ensure, guarantee, deliver)
- [ ] Are there unnecessary intensifiers? (very, highly, extremely, deeply)
- [ ] Are there buzzwords? (cutting-edge, innovative, game-changing, disruptive, holistic)
- [ ] Does data speak for itself, or is it dressed up with adjectives?
- [ ] Does the opening lead with purpose rather than a claim?
- [ ] Does the closing reflect and commit, not boast?
- [ ] Are sentences active and under 25 words where possible?
- [ ] Are there any em-dashes (—)? Remove all instances. Replace with a colon, a full stop, a comma or reword the sentence.
- [ ] Is there any self-congratulatory tone, even subtle? Remove it.
- [ ] Would this copy feel appropriate in a communication from a government ministry or professional standards body?
